The ambition

More than a beverage destination.

We want T-REPÜBLIK to become a lifestyle brand people recognise, crave and come back to. Product desire, a memorable place, social connection and contemporary visual culture, working together.

What the social presence has to do
  • CraveCreate product cravings and push people to discover more of the menu.
  • Stand apartLook and feel different from a conventional café.
  • DestinationPosition the venue as a social place people want to be seen in.
  • BelongBuild emotional connection and a real community.
  • DemystifyMake bubble tea easy and inviting for first-timers.
  • RitualiseTie the brand to everyday rituals and to special occasions.
  • RecogniseBuild an identity you spot instantly, without every post looking the same.

Three visual territories

Three ways of looking. One brand.

These are overarching creative principles, not extra content pillars. Every post draws on at least one of them; the strongest draw on two.

Territory A

Product Obsession

Make the product impossible to ignore. Sensory photography, experimental compositions, ingredient stories, texture and colours taken straight from the cup.

Territory B

The Republik Experience

The atmosphere, architecture, light and small details of the actual venue, and how it feels to spend time there.

Territory C

People & Play

People together. Social rituals, personality, humour, celebrations and the lifestyle culture around the drink.

Interconnected, not separate

Each pillar has a home territory, and the most useful pillars live in the overlaps. Boba 101 teaches through product and people. Moments joins people to the place. The brand itself sits at the centre, where all three meet.

Family 01 · Taro

Taro, in purple.

Creamy, earthy, unmistakably taro.

Purple is allowed to take over everything here: the backdrop, the ice, the type. We move from texture (pearls sinking into foam, ice in purple milk) to the hero cup, then to a split layout and an ingredient card that tells first-timers what is inside.

Sampled from the executions

Family 02 · Brown Sugar & Crème Brûlée

The first crack.

Creamy caramel with a brûlée twist.

This family is a sensory sequence, so we shoot it like one: the torch, the crack of caramelised sugar, the milk pour, the layers taken apart in mid-air, then the finished glass on the table. Caramel and espresso browns, warm side light and fluted glass carry the whole story.

Sampled from the executions

Family 03 · Matcha

Matcha & friends.

Where the product green meets the venue green.

Matcha is the one family whose colour already belongs to the venue, so it bridges product and place. We show it as a family (Matcha, Matcha Strawberry, Matcha Mango) and let the layered gradient become its signature: green on top, milk in the middle, fruit at the base.

Sampled from the executions

Family 04 · Butterfly Pea

Violet into gold.

The most theatrical drink on the menu.

Butterfly pea turns from deep violet to gold, so the pictures are about the gradient. Butterfly pea flowers, pineapple, mint and soft afternoon light make it the menu's most romantic family. We keep the props delicate so the colour change stays the star.

Sampled from the executions

Family 05 · Beyond boba

The rest of the menu.

Bingsu, refreshers, lattes, espresso.

The same rules apply beyond bubble tea: a serif name, a three-word line, the product in warm directional light against the venue's deep green. These cards keep the wider menu inside one visual family.

No type at all. Texture, light and the product, so the craving comes first.

Pillar 02 · Lifestyle & Atmosphere

Worth the trip.

بيستاهل المشوار

Strategic objective

Position T-REPÜBLIK as a destination worth visiting, not simply a place to pick up a drink. The concept: A Day at T-REPÜBLIK, told through the light as it moves across the real venue.

Sunlight

Scene 1 · Sunlight

Leaf shadow on the wall.

Natural sunlight, architectural details and material textures. The brand appears the way a visitor first meets it: a black T on a sunlit wall, a cup on a terrazzo table, a quiet chair.

Scene 2 · Afternoon rituals

The long afternoon.

Friends, sunglasses, an extra cookie. Afternoon is when the terrace fills up, so we photograph the ritual of staying, not the transaction of buying.

Scene 3 · Golden hour

When the sky turns pink.

A brown sugar cup, a warm table lamp, the sun dropping behind the trees. Golden hour is the venue's most cinematic light; it deserves its own recurring slot.

Scene 4 · Evening

Lanterns on.

Paper lanterns, a glowing T, candlelit tables on the terrace. The evening shows a second personality of the same place, calmer and more intimate.

Grounded in the real place

The moodboard sets the feeling. The venue sets the facts.

These frames from the venue's own channel are reference only. They tell production what is really there, so every future shoot stays recognisably T-REPÜBLIK.

  • Façade: green and cream vertical cladding with the rePÜBLIK wordmark and "refreshments reimagined."
  • Terrace: woven rope chairs with sage cushions, stone-top tables, black branded umbrellas.
  • View: an open terrace facing the hillside city, which no other café can copy.
  • Light: linear light posts and a strong blue hour, a natural next scene for the evening story.

Future production approach

Photograph the community, don't cast it.

  1. Cast from the counterInvite real regulars and staff, with written consent for every person who appears.
  2. Shoot on locationAlways at the venue: terrace, bar and entrance, in real light at real hours.
  3. Documentary firstCandid moments and natural expressions before poses. Let people be themselves.
  4. Fashion-editorial portraitsA contemporary portrait style for Republik Faces, with styling kept to what people actually wear.
  5. Direct flash after darkHard on-camera flash for evening portraits and nights out, where it suits the energy.
  6. Their words, not oursCaptions quote the person. Copy stays light so the face carries the post.
Why no images This chapter is intentionally typographic. Its photography will come from real people at T-REPÜBLIK, not from generated or stock stand-ins.

Typography

Three voices appear across the approved work. Each has one job. Final typefaces are set in the brand guideline; the specimens here show the role, not the font file.

Voice 1 · Editorial serif
Taro Milk Tea

Product names, signature drinks and campaign headlines. Elegant, high contrast, often with an italic partner.

Seen in · Taro, Crème Brûlée, Birthdays
Voice 2 · Bold grotesk
Meet the Boba Family

Education and information. Short, heavy, readable at a glance in Stories.

Seen in · Boba 101 series
Voice 3 · Handwritten
Him · Her ♡

Human notes on top of photographs: names, arrows, the "Boba 101" tag. Used sparingly.

Seen in · Boba Date, Boba 101 tags
  • Two voices maximum per post. Serif with a handwritten note, or grotesk with a handwritten tag. Never all three.
  • Type never covers the product. It sits behind, beside or above the cup.
  • Ghosted type is for experiments. Large faded serif behind a product belongs to the 25% experimental slot.
  • Arabic gets the same care. Arabic headlines get their own typeface pairing with matching weight and contrast, never a direct swap.
  • One short line. Three words or one sentence of copy on the image. Everything else goes in the caption.
  • Spell the name one way. T-REPÜBLIK in brand lockups; never "T-Republic" in on-image text.

Photography

What every frame shares, whichever pillar it belongs to.

  • Directional natural lightLow sun, leaf shadows, warm side light. Hard flash only for evening portraits.
  • The vessel is consistentClear branded cups and fluted glass. The black straw and the T sticker are always in frame.
  • Real surfacesStone, terrazzo, steel at the bar, the venue's own furniture. No seamless studio paper for lifestyle.
  • Texture up closeEvery product family gets at least one macro: pearls, foam, ice, caramel crust.
  • People are candidNatural expressions, real interactions, hands at work. Posing only when it is clearly a joke.
  • Colour from the subjectBackgrounds pick up the product's own colour or the hour's light, never an arbitrary tint.

Constant vs. flexible

The rule that lets each post look different while the feed still reads as one brand.

Always the same

  • The black-and-white T mark
  • Clear cup, black straw, fluted glass
  • Natural, directional light
  • The product as the hero
  • The real venue as the setting

Free to change

  • Background colour, led by the product
  • Composition: macro, split, deconstructed, collage
  • Typography voice
  • Time of day, from sunlight to evening
  • Mood: playful, editorial, intimate, celebratory
